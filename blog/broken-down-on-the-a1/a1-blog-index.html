Broken Down on the A1 in the North East? Here's What to Do.

By CW Vehicle Transport & Recovery Newcastle & North East Updated July 2026
If you are in immediate danger

Call 999 first. If you are on a live carriageway or cannot get to safety, your priority is emergency services, not recovery. Everything below assumes you are out of immediate danger.

The A1 is one of the busiest roads in the North East. It runs through the heart of the region, from the Tyne Bridge in Gateshead south through Durham and Darlington, carrying tens of thousands of vehicles every day. If something goes wrong on it, the next few minutes matter.

This guide covers exactly what to do if you break down on the A1 between Newcastle and Darlington, how to stay safe while you wait, and how to get recovered as quickly as possible.

Step by step: what to do the moment you break down

  • 1
    Put your hazard lights on immediately

    Do this the moment you feel something is wrong, even before you have fully stopped. It warns traffic behind you and buys you time. Do not wait until you have pulled over.

  • 2
    Pull over as far left as possible

    If you can move the car, get it onto the hard shoulder or as far left as the road allows. Try to stop on a straight section rather than a bend so drivers behind can see you earlier. Avoid stopping on a hill or bridge if you can help it.

  • 3
    Get out of the passenger side only

    Never get out on the driver's side. Open the passenger door and exit that way, then move your passengers out the same way. The driver's side faces live traffic.

  • 4
    Get behind the barrier and away from the car

    Move as far from the vehicle as possible, ideally behind the crash barrier or up a verge. Do not sit in the car while it is on the hard shoulder or at the roadside. Stationary vehicles are struck from behind far more often than most people expect.

  • 5
    Put a warning triangle out if it is safe to do so

    If you have one and can place it safely behind your vehicle without stepping into traffic, do so. If it would put you at risk to place it, leave it. Your safety is more important than warning other drivers.

  • 6
    Call for recovery

    Once you are safely away from the vehicle, call a recovery service. Have your location ready. On the A1 in the North East, the most useful landmarks are junction numbers, the nearest town name, and which direction you are travelling.

Is the A1 in the North East a motorway?

Parts of it are, and parts are not, which catches people out. The stretch around Gateshead and into Durham is a dual carriageway classified as the A1(M) in places, which does have motorway rules. Other sections are standard A-road. In practice, treat the whole route the same way for safety: get off the carriageway, get behind the barrier, and do not walk along the road.

On a motorway, you must not use the road for anything other than driving, which includes stopping unless in an emergency. On the non-motorway sections you have slightly more flexibility, but the safety principles are identical.

How to tell where you are on the A1

If you are not sure exactly where you are, here is how to figure it out quickly:

  • Look for junction numbers or signs on the road. The A1 in the North East has clearly numbered junctions which help recovery drivers locate you fast.
  • Check your phone's maps app. Even with no data, GPS usually still works and will show your coordinates or nearest road.
  • Note the direction of travel: northbound towards Newcastle, or southbound towards Darlington and beyond.
  • Look for landmarks: Chester Services, Bowburn, Leeming Bar, the Angel of the North junction are all recognisable points along the route.

What if the car cannot move?

If the vehicle will not start or cannot be driven safely to the side of the road, stay calm. Switch on your hazard lights, get out the passenger side and behind the barrier, and call recovery immediately. Tell them the car is in a live lane or partially blocking the road so they can prioritise accordingly.

Do not attempt to push the car yourself on a live carriageway. It is not worth the risk. A good recovery driver can position the truck to load a non-runner safely without you needing to be near the vehicle.

What if it happens at night?

The A1 at night is quieter but faster. Drivers travel at higher speeds with reduced reaction times. If you break down after dark:

  • Keep your hazard lights on at all times. They are your main warning signal.
  • Wear a hi-vis jacket if you have one in the car. Many people carry one and forget about it until exactly this moment.
  • Stay well behind the barrier, further back than you think you need to. At night drivers sometimes do not see a stationary vehicle until they are very close.
  • Use your phone torch to make yourself visible if you need to flag someone down, but only do so from behind the barrier.

Common reasons for breaking down on the A1

Most A1 breakdowns fall into a handful of categories. Knowing the signs early can save you from a full roadside stop:

  • Tyre blowout or puncture. If you feel a sudden pull to one side or hear a loud bang followed by a heavy dragging, a tyre has gone. Do not brake sharply. Ease off the accelerator gently, grip the wheel firmly, and steer smoothly to the left.
  • Engine overheating. Temperature gauge in the red, steam from the bonnet, burning smell. Pull over, switch off, and do not open the bonnet until the engine has cooled. Opening it while hot can cause serious burns.
  • Flat battery. Car cuts out without warning, dashboard lights come on. A jump start may fix it but if the battery keeps failing it needs replacing before you drive far.
  • Fuel running out. More common than people admit, especially on longer runs. If you run dry on the A1, do not try to roll to a junction. Pull over safely and call for help.
  • Alternator failure. Battery warning light comes on while driving, electrical systems start going wrong. The car will keep running for a short while but get to safety quickly.

In an emergency, yes, but you should not leave it any longer than necessary. Stationary vehicles on the hard shoulder are a serious hazard to other drivers. Call recovery immediately and make sure your hazards are on and the car is as far left as possible.
